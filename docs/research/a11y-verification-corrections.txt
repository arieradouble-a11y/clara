[Legal & regulatory drivers for operators] OVERSTATED: AI Act Art 50(5) is the strongest and most immediate lever and already binds AI chat providers; it attaches to Art 50(1) systems 'intended to interact directly with natural persons' — i.e. every consumer AI chat UI — regardless of risk classification. → AI Act Art 50 transparency obligations have applied since 2 August 2026 (unchanged by the Digital Omnibus, which added only a grace period to 2 December 2026 for machine-readable marking of systems already on the market). Art 50(5) requires that the disclosure information 'conform to the applicable accessibility requirements'. But its reach is narrower than claimed on three counts: (a) Art 50(1) expressly does NOT apply 'unless this is obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect, taking into account the circumstances and the context of use' — for a standalone, clearly-branded consumer AI chat product the obviousness carve-out plausibly disapplies the duty entirely; (b) Art 50(5) attaches accessibility only to 'the information referred to in paragraphs 1 to 4', i.e. the AI-interaction notice and content markings, not to the chat interface, its outputs, or the product as a whole; (c) 'applicable accessibility requirements' is a renvoi to other instruments (Recital 132 / the EAA and WAD), so it inherits their scope limits rather than creating a freestanding accessibility duty.
